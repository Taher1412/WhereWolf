# Under trees, paths and bearings beat echoes

Pothenot's core loop (clock bearings to tall landmarks, the sun as compass and information-gain questions) carries over to the Bois de Vincennes. Its street-tuned audio cues do not, and several of them will actively push the posterior out of the woods. The Bois is an unusually easy forest. It is a fragmented park-forest where **the median wooded cell is only 25 m from a forest edge, 77% of the park lies within 25 m of a mapped way**, and in late November the deciduous stands are mostly leafless, so the Château keep and the low sun stay visible from many spots ([OSM relation 18047835](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/18047835), measured via Overpass). The building-echo matcher has nothing to match there. Forest impulse responses are diffuse, noise-like tails with no specular spikes ([Kaneko & Gamper 2021](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2021/10/A-fast-forest-reverberator-using-single-scattering-cylinders-MMSP-2021.pdf)), and the current code scores a wooded spot that returns three unmatched trunk echoes *below* a spot inside a building. The road-noise model has no vegetation term, although woodland removes roughly **5–10 dB per 100 m** ([Van Renterghem 2014](https://users.ugent.be/~tvrenter/publicaties/guidelinestreebelts.pdf); [Bucur](https://cdn.homeownershub.com/media/73165/60a02322623617.28914949.pdf)). Visibility is a fixed radius, so "I can't see the keep" from under trees is read as "far from the keep". The grid itself stops at 48.830 N, which leaves the southern **1.4 km of the 3.2 km-deep park** (the Gravelle side) off the map. The fixes are cheap and mostly not acoustic: widen the box, gate the echo matcher by land cover, wire the already-built `backend/forest.py` layers into constraints and questions, replace radius visibility with a canopy ray model from the IGN LiDAR canopy-height raster, and add the phone compass and shadow questions for when the sun is hidden. In the forest the chirp is reduced to one honest job, a "you are inside woodland" label, and it is worth keeping only if a 15-minute field test confirms it.

## Four street-tuned cues misfire under trees today

The repository already contains a forest rasteriser. `backend/forest.py` builds per-cell `wood`, `open` and `water` shares plus distances to paths, paved and dirt ways, the wood edge, water, open ground and fences. **Nothing else in the backend calls it**, so none of these layers reach `localise.py` or `questions.py` (repo grep, this session). The cues that *are* wired up were designed for street canyons, and four of them will behave badly in the Bois.

The echo matcher is the most serious. `surround.py` multiplies every measured echo that matches no mapped wall by `ECHO_FA = 0.25`, tempers the result by 0.8, and gives spots inside buildings a flat `ECHO_INDOOR = 0.08`. A wooded spot has no footprint walls, so if the peak picker reports three trunk returns, that spot scores 0.25³ raised to 0.8, or **0.036, less than half the value given to a spot inside a building**, while a street spot that happens to have walls at those ranges scores near 1. Trunk echoes are physically plausible. With a random stem layout the nearest trunk sits at 0.5/√n, about 2.5 m at 400 stems/ha, and so echoes at about 15 ms. A single 30 cm trunk returns roughly **18 dB less than a facade** at 5 m (geometric estimate in the research notes, not measured). The likely failure is that every forest report gets pulled toward the nearest buildings. Whether the peak picker actually emits trunk peaks has to be checked in the field test below.

The setting classifier is next. Measured bistatic forest impulse responses are reverberant: Koli birch and spruce forest gives **T30 of 1.3–1.7 s in the 1 kHz octave**, "comparable with that of a small church", with C50 of 15–40 dB ([Shelley et al. 2013](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/78001/1/56.dafx2013_submission_26.pdf)), and real forest IRs have "nearly constant echo density profiles" from the first milliseconds ([Kaneko & Gamper 2021](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2021/10/A-fast-forest-reverberator-using-single-scattering-cylinders-MMSP-2021.pdf)). `spatial.py` models every outdoor setting with a median apparent RT60 of 0.12–0.28 s, so a reverberant forest tail is evidence for `indoor_large`. A phone's speaker and mic sit 10–15 cm apart, so it only hears weak backscatter, and its tail will be shorter than the published figures. No monostatic phone IR in a forest has been published, though, so the size of the bias is unknown.

The road-noise map is the third. It spreads sources as lines, applies 0.4 dB/100 m of air absorption, and screens by 15 dB only where a building blocks the ray, or by a blanket 10.5 dB beyond 250 m. Woodland adds attenuation that the map ignores. Belts under 20–30 m deep add almost nothing. About 30 m of heavy wood is needed for **5 dB(A)**, and stands reach **7–10 dB per 100 m** at 1.2–3.9 m receiver height ([Van Renterghem 2014](https://users.ugent.be/~tvrenter/publicaties/guidelinestreebelts.pdf); [Bucur](https://cdn.homeownershub.com/media/73165/60a02322623617.28914949.pdf)). Prediction schemes are known to underestimate it: Huisman and Attenborough found pine-forest attenuation "considerably underpredicted" by the Nordic scheme ([abstract](https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/993718255)). Inside 250 m of a road, then, the map over-predicts forest cells by roughly 5–12 dB. Beyond 250 m the blanket 10.5 dB happens to approximate 200 m of wood by coincidence.

Visibility is the fourth. `localise.py` turns "I can't see the keep" into a penalty on every cell inside a soft 1,500 m radius. That is close to the whole northern Bois. Under canopy, "can't see" mostly means "trees in the way", not "far away", so the term pushes the posterior in the wrong direction. Beneath all four sits the bounding box. `config.BBOX` runs from 48.830 to 48.865 N, while the park relation runs from 48.8171 to 48.8459 N ([OSM](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/18047835)). The southern 1.4 km of the park, including the Plateau and Lac de Gravelle side, cannot hold the casualty at all.

## Woodland map data is rich, but "on a path" barely localises

Two open sources cover what a hackathon needs, and each downloads in under two minutes. An Overpass pull clipped to the park returned **973 ha** of park, with forest polygons over **44%** of cells and leaf-type tags on about half of them. It also returned 2,879 highway ways (107 km of `path`, 62 km of `footway`, 32 km of `track`, 21 km of `bridleway`), surface tags on 1,628 of those ways, 75 water ways plus 12 water relations, 186 fence or wall ways, 184 sports features, 620 benches and 11,772 tree nodes. The full geometry came to 6.3 MB of JSON ([OSM relation 18047835](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/18047835), measured 2026-11-21). The tree nodes are dominated by Tilia and Platanus, typical of alignment trees. They are probably the City of Paris inventory, which explicitly **excludes trees planted as forest stands in the bois** ([data.gouv.fr: Les arbres](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/les-arbres-paris)), so they mark avenues rather than the forest interior. The IGN LiDAR HD canopy-height model is open under Licence Ouverte 2.0 ([data.gouv.fr: MNH LiDAR HD](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/mnh-lidar-hd)). A single Géoplateforme WMS GetMap call with `FORMAT=image/geotiff` returned the whole park at about 5 m pixels in about 2.6 MB, with no restricted-zone holes. Inside OSM forest, the canopy-height median is **11.5 m** and the 90th percentile 18 m. Terrain spans only about 48–73 m, which makes slope a weak cue everywhere except around Gravelle.

The measured statistics overturn one intuitive recommendation. The phone-sensor research ranked a "you are on a path" constraint first. In Vincennes, though, **53% of cells lie within 12.5 m of a mapped way and 77% within 25 m**, so presence on a path is nearly free information. What discriminates is the *kind* of path and the features around it. The table gives first-question gains under a uniform prior, computed as IG = H(q) − H(ε) for a symmetric answer error ε, which is an upper bound. ε itself is assumed, because no study of how reliably people report these features was found.

| Spoken cue | Share of park cells "yes" | Bits at 0% / 10% / 20% answer error |
|---|---|---|
| Under trees (OSM forest) | 0.44 | 0.99 / 0.52 / 0.27 |
| Water within 200 m (an underestimate: water ways only) | 0.31 | 0.89 / 0.46 / 0.24 |
| Main road within about 100 m | 0.27 | 0.84 / 0.43 / 0.22 |
| Unpaved surface underfoot | 0.24 | 0.80 / 0.40 / 0.21 |
| On asphalt or a road | 0.17 | 0.66 / 0.32 / 0.16 |
| Conifers around you | ~0.14 | 0.58 / 0.28 / 0.14 |

The arithmetic matters for the ask-back loop. Shrinking a 300 m radius to 60 m takes about 4.6 bits, so three noisy yes/no answers at about 0.5 bit each cannot get there. The forest questions therefore need two upgrades. First, ask **multi-way** questions: "what is under your feet: tarmac, gravel or packed earth, sand, or no path?" carries an estimated 0.6–1.0 bit. Fill missing surface tags by class (service and tertiary as asphalt, track and bridleway as compacted, path as dirt) with a wider σ. Second, ask **bearing-carrying** questions: "can you hear heavy traffic, and at which clock direction?" or "is the road at your 3 and 9 o'clock?" turn a line feature into a bearing that constrains position and heading together. Doctrine supports exactly these forms. Handrails, catching features and attack points are standard land-navigation aids, and terrain association is "more forgiving of mistakes and far less time-consuming than dead reckoning" ([TC 3-25.26](https://armyrotc.mst.edu/media/academic/armyrotc/documents/manualsadpadrpfmetc/TC%203-25.26.pdf)). SAR experience points the same way: injured lost people are usually found close to a linear feature ([Popular Science](https://www.popsci.com/environment/the-burgeoning-science-of-search-and-rescue/)). That justifies a mild path-proximity prior, `0.3 + 0.7·exp(−d_way/25 m)`, in civilian mode. In tactical mode it should be off, because soldiers take cover in tree lines.

Visibility is the highest-value map fix, because it repairs every existing landmark question inside the wood. Airborne LiDAR sees the canopy, not the trunks that block eye-level sightlines. A trunk-obstruction model underestimated target visibility by only **8.8%**, while a mean-vegetation-height surface underestimated it by **65.7%** ([Murgoitio et al. 2013](https://scienceportal.tecnalia.com/en/publications/improved-visibility-calculations-with-tree-trunk-obstruction-mode/)). The practical form is Beer–Lambert. Sample the ray from each cell to each of the roughly 50 tall landmarks on the canopy raster, sum the length s where canopy exceeds 5 m, and set P_vis = exp(−s/λ). Start with λ at 100–150 m leaf-off, and let tall targets stay visible over the canopy from clearings. Then multiply the bearing term by P_vis for "I see X", and use 1 − P_vis for "I can't see X". The precompute is about 78 million raster lookups, a few seconds in numpy. No published leaf-on or leaf-off sightline distance in metres was found for deciduous woodland. Gresham et al. even found no summer/winter difference in Welsh small-stem woods, from only 8 rescanned plots ([Gresham et al. 2023](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/114066/1/Ecology%20and%20Evolution%20-%202023%20-%20Gresham%20-%20Horizontal%20viewsheds%20of%20large%20herbivores%20as%20a%20function%20of%20woodland%20structure.pdf)). λ is therefore a field-calibrated constant, not a literature value.

## Forest sound gives a land-cover label and rough road distance, not geometry

Passive listening keeps some value under trees, but in a different form. Woodland adds **5–10 dB per 100 m** of excess attenuation, mostly from the soft forest floor (flow resistivity about 20 kPa·s·m⁻² against 300 for grass) and from multiple scattering off trunks. Foliage contributes little at traffic frequencies, and leaf-off costs up to about **3 dB(A)** ([Van Renterghem 2014](https://users.ugent.be/~tvrenter/publicaties/guidelinestreebelts.pdf); [Forest Research](https://cdn.forestresearch.gov.uk/2025/05/TDAG_FirstStepsUrbanNoise.AW_.pdf)). Day-to-day weather did not change 100 m forest attenuation significantly ([Huisman & Attenborough](https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/993718255)), which makes the forest term more stable than long open-field paths. A calibrated road map with this term could tell "near Route de la Pyramide" from "deep in a stand". With phone level error of **±2 dB at best on iOS**, and only 4 of 62 Android apps partly meeting the criteria ([NIOSH 2014](https://cdc.gov/niosh/blogs/2014/sound-app.html)), it remains a σ ≈ 6 dB term. Two calibration-free features help separate a near small road from a far motorway at the same dB(A). One is the traffic-band tilt L(125–500 Hz) − L(1–4 kHz), since air absorbs about **3–4 dB per 100 m at 4 kHz** ([DTIC ADA588823](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA588823.pdf)). The other is an NDSI-style ratio, which in Thetford Forest tracked distance to the nearest road with **r² ≈ 0.37–0.40** and crossed zero about 1 km from a busy road ([Turner et al. 2018](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0189843)). Neither has a published traffic-ranging curve, and Thetford was recorded on summer mornings. Late-November Paris has little biophony, so the NDSI will mostly measure traffic, and bird cues are an occasional bonus. Single BirdNET detections are unreliable even in cities, where per-species precision ranges from 0.29 to above 0.7 ([BirdNET in a European city](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395419520_BirdNET_can_be_as_good_as_experts_for_acoustic_bird_monitoring_in_a_European_city)).

The chirp should change jobs. Building-footprint matching has nothing to work with in a forest. Sparse, strong specular spikes mean facades, while a dense, smoothly decaying, high-echo-density envelope means trunks, and that difference is exactly what separates woodland from street. The research proposes five cheap features on the existing deconvolved IR: tail energy relative to the ground echo, envelope kurtosis over 10–200 ms (low for forest, high for streets), normalised echo density, the T(1 kHz)/T(4 kHz) ratio (forest peaks near 1 kHz because trunk diameters are comparable to the wavelength, per [Shelley et al.](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/78001/1/56.dafx2013_submission_26.pdf)), and the earliest reflection delay. A rule classifier on these features, mapped to `forest.py`'s `wood` share, would remove streets, lawns and the lakes from the hypotheses. Nobody has published its accuracy. A second use is speculative. In a clearing, the forest edge should appear as a rise in diffuse energy at t = 2D/c (117 ms for 20 m), which gives a distance-to-edge likelihood that `d_edge` already supports. Backscatter at 60–800 Hz is organised at a real pine edge ([Swearingen et al. 2007](https://acoustics.org/pressroom/httpdocs/153rd/swearingen.html)), but no collocated phone measurement exists, and the link budget is marginal beyond about 20 m. Per-trunk diameters and stem density are not recoverable from one chirp. Even ultrasonic bat sonar recovers one foliage parameter only when the others are known ([PMC5560571](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5560571)).

The forest also changes the OPSEC arithmetic, though not enough to make an audible sweep acceptable. Habitat attenuation follows an exponential law, and detection distance "mostly depends on the ambient sound", varying up to **fivefold** over the day and seasons ([Haupert et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16077)). An 85 dB phone sweep against 35 dB woodland background with about 0.2 dB/m excess loss falls to background at roughly **65–70 m**, against about 300 m in the open. That is a back-of-envelope figure built on assumed inputs, and it remains a larger footprint than the spoken report. The chirp should stay opt-in, band-limited to 0.5–2 kHz and quiet, with the passive voice-decay estimate as the zero-emission fallback.

## The phone itself restores heading when the sun hides

Under canopy the sun is intermittent, and that weakens the cue that collapses the 72-heading dimension. On 21 November at 48.84 N, the repo's `sun.py` puts the sun at a **maximum elevation of 21.4° around 12:40**, with azimuth moving 13–15° per hour, and setting near 17:00. Low sun enters under the crowns from the side and casts shadows about 2.5 times object height at noon, which gives two cheap substitutes. "Which way do shadows point?" is the existing sun term with a 180° offset and σ ≈ 20°. "Which side of the trees is bright?" gives a σ ≈ 45° sun bearing when the disc is hidden. Both are engineering choices, since no study quantified sun visibility under late-autumn canopy. Under intact canopies, sunflecks last only **4–6 minutes** ([Canham et al.](https://andrewsforest.oregonstate.edu/sites/default/files/lter/pubs/pdf/pub1134.pdf)), so the report must be timestamped.

The magnetometer is the main new sensor, used with honest error bars. In the one controlled three-phone study found, median azimuth RMSE was **22–24° outdoors and static**, rising to **45.6° with a 10–15 µT soft-iron disturbance** and 41.9° when the phone was moving. The maximum outdoor error was 50.8° ([Elias & Maas 2020](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/V-1-2020/181/2020/isprs-annals-V-1-2020-181-2020.pdf)). A plate carrier or rifle next to the phone is exactly that disturbance. Both target browsers expose a heading. On iOS it is `webkitCompassHeading` after `DeviceOrientationEvent.requestPermission()` from a tap. On Chrome it is `deviceorientationabsolute` ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/DeviceOrientationEvent/requestPermission_static); [W3C draft](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/WD-orientation-event-20241024/)). It is best used two ways. As a heading prior with σ = 25° (35° if the reported accuracy is poor), it cuts 72 headings to an effective 10–15. Pointing the phone at a landmark and tapping is stronger, because each pointed landmark becomes an absolute line of position independent of θ, so two pointed landmarks give a fix with no sun at all. Pitch and roll are accurate to under 2° and barely affected by metal ([Elias & Maas 2020](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/V-1-2020/181/2020/isprs-annals-V-1-2020-181-2020.pdf)), so a phone laid flat measures slope. In terrain with only about 25 m of total relief that buys little.

Dead reckoning matters more on the first report than any sensor. Phone PDR alone ran to **174.8 m RMS** over 1.2–5.6 km walks. A barometer matched against a 10 m DEM cut that to 45.8 m, but SRTM30 did not help at all ([Broyles et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6068906/)), and no browser exposes the barometer ([Chrome Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/capabilities/web-apis/generic-sensor)). The cheap version is spoken and stored. Cache the last good `navigator.geolocation` fix with its timestamp, then bound the prior to a disk of radius v_max·Δt plus the fix accuracy: 12 minutes at 1.4 m/s is about 1 km, against 995 ha for the whole Bois. Parse "ten minutes north from the car park" into a log-normal range (σ ≈ 45%, a guess, since no source quantifies recalled walking time) times a bearing sector. Live step counting is fragile, because sensor readings only arrive while the page is visible, and a medic will not keep the field page open while moving.

The ranking below weighs effort against how much each change moves the posterior in the Bois. Items 1–4 are about five hours together. They fix outright errors and reuse layers that already exist. None of the gains is measured, so each should be checked with the synthetic test harness at Bois test points, counting questions to 75 m and final error with and without the change.

| Test | Protocol | Calibrates |
|---|---|---|
| A. Chirp scenes (15 min) | 5 IRs inside a stand, 5 in a clearing at taped 10, 20 and 40 m from the edge, 5 on a street | Whether trunk peaks appear (item 2), forest classifier thresholds (item 10), edge onset |
| B. Traffic transect (20 min) | 20 s clips every 50 m walking into the wood from the Route de la Pyramide or the A4 edge, raw audio with all processing off | Forest dB/m, tilt coefficients, σ (item 9) |

The limits are real and should be stated in the pitch. The Bois flatters the method. It is cut into small stands by a dense, well-mapped path network, and it has a 50 m-class keep and a leafless canopy in November. A continuous forest with sparse OSM paths and no tall landmarks would leave Pothenot with heading cues and vague priors. No published smartphone impulse response exists from inside a forest. No validated sightline distance exists for leaf-off deciduous woodland. No study reports how reliably people describe surface, tree type or distance to an edge. Every coefficient marked as assumed above therefore stays a placeholder until tests A–E are done.

## Conclusion

The forest flips the earlier finding that "sound narrows the search, landmarks pin it". Under trees, the *map* does the narrowing, and sound mostly answers one land-cover question. The leverage is in the digital twin the team already holds: a canopy raster that says where a landmark can be seen, path-surface and water layers that turn vague answers into bits, and a land-cover mask that keeps the street-echo machinery from voting where it has no evidence. That makes the most important forest work defensive. The single change most likely to rescue a forest demo is the hour that stops trunk echoes from reading as missing buildings, not a new sensor.

There is also a pitch argument hidden in the physics. Woodland is where GPS-free reporting matters and where the street-scale competitors have nothing to say, and Pothenot's information-gain dialogue is the one component that transfers unchanged. Bearing-carrying forest questions ("traffic at which o'clock?", "point at the keep") and a canopy-aware "can you see it?" turn that dialogue into a forest product. The claim stays honest only if the number quoted on Sunday comes from test E in the Bois, not from the synthetic harness.
