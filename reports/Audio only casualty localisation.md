# Sound narrows the search, landmarks pin it

Short phone audio alone cannot place a casualty to street level. The published state of the art says so plainly. The best 2025 audio geolocation model has a **median error of about 1,082 km** and puts only **6.4% of clips within 25 km** ([arXiv 2505.18726](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18726v2)). Ambient fingerprinting works only at room or store level, and only against places someone has already recorded ([Tarzia et al. 2011](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~pdinda/Papers/mobisys11.pdf)). What audio does well is answer three cheap questions: what kind of place this is (indoor, street canyon, open park), which mapped sources are within earshot (surface rail, a ringing church, a main road), and whether a hard surface is within a few metres. Each answer becomes a soft term on Pothenot's existing 25 m likelihood grid. Combined, they turn "anywhere in 4x4 km" into a handful of candidate areas, and the spoken landmarks then pin the position down. The approximate 3D model of the surroundings is better predicted per cell from the IGN height raster and then matched to coarse audio categories than built from the audio itself. Single-mic room shape recovery is not achievable from audio, and even phone sonar gives distances without bearings. The highest-value work for the remaining day is not new acoustics. It is making the existing evidence robust (tempered, floored, de-duplicated likelihoods), letting passive listening run while the buddy does first aid, and replacing yes/no follow-ups with clock-bearing questions that carry several bits each. Together these get most reports to 0–1 questions.

## Room acoustics give a three-way setting label, not geometry

Blind reverberation estimation from speech is mature, but only within its training conditions. On the real ACE corpus, the best single-channel CNNs estimate fullband T60 from 4 s of speech with **MSE ≈ 0.026 s² (RMSE ≈ 0.16 s) and correlation 0.92** ([Bryan 2019, arXiv 1909.03642](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.03642.pdf)). An earlier 2,541-parameter CNN reached MSE 0.038 s² and ρ 0.84, against a train-set MSE of 0.0125, which shows a large gap when generalising to unseen rooms ([Gamper & Tashev 2018](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2018/09/Blind_reverberation_time_estimation_Gamper_IWAENC_2018.pdf)). Blind DRR is much weaker, at **MSE ≈ 9 dB² and ρ ≈ 0.71** at best ([Bryan 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.03642.pdf)). In practice, ±0.2 s on T60 cleanly separates dry or outdoor (<0.2 s), a furnished room (0.3–0.6 s) and a hard hall, stairwell or tunnel (>1 s), and little finer. That matches the four-class setting classifier in `backend/spatial.py`.

Close talk breaks these models. Every one of them was trained on talkers 1–3 m from the mic, with DRR between −6 and +18 dB ([Bryan 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.03642.pdf)). The critical-distance formula d_c ≈ 0.1·√(QV/πT60) ([Genovese et al. 2019](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2019/04/preprint_ICASSP2019_VolumeEstimation.pdf)) puts d_c near 0.8 m for a 50 m³ room. With a mouth at 10 cm, DRR is then around +18 dB or more: the reverberant tail is buried and the estimators read "dry". This explains the downward T60 bias the team already saw. Making the voice-based estimate one-sided was the right patch. Better fixes are to estimate decay from non-voice excitation (the chirp, a clap, a door), from ambient-only segments, or from free-decay windows right after word endings.

Room volume and shape are out of reach. Blind volume from 4 s of noisy speech is accurate only to about a factor of two: **MeanMult 1.75** with an attention model ([arXiv 2309.13504](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.13504v3)), and **3.3 on the noisy real ACE corpus** for the earlier CNN, which also struggled with rooms under 400 m³ ([Genovese et al. 2019](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2019/04/preprint_ICASSP2019_VolumeEstimation.pdf)). Recovering room shape from echoes needs several microphones and a known emission ([Dokmanić et al., PNAS 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3725052)). No single-mic, blind method for room dimensions with real-room accuracy turned up in the research.

Passive indoor/outdoor classification works well on urban scenes. The DCASE 2020 three-class winner reached **96.5% overall**, but only **92.7% on "indoor"**, its weakest class ([DCASE 2020 Task 1B](https://dcase.community/challenge2020/task-acoustic-scene-classification-results-b)). A mismatched recording device costs about 10 points (70.4% vs 59.6% on the 2018 task) ([DCASE 2018](https://dcase.community/challenge2018/task-acoustic-scene-classification)). None of these classes covers tree lines, ruins or rural ground, so the YAMNet scene mapping is unvalidated exactly where a military user would be.

Phone hardware sets the ceiling. Android only guarantees an unprocessed mic to within **±10 dB from 100 Hz to 7 kHz and ±30 dB from 7 to 22 kHz** ([Android CDD 5.11](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/compatibility/cdd/+/refs/tags/android-9.0.0_r9/5_multimedia/5_11_unprocessed-audio.md)). Speaker-to-mic round-trip latency can be up to **300 ms** ([Android 15 CDD](https://source.android.com/docs/compatibility/15/android-15-cdd)). Chrome's `echoCancellation:false` historically disables WebRTC processing but leaves hardware DSP alone ([Chrome blog](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/disabling-hardware-noise-suppression/)), and Safari has long ignored some constraints and kept a lowpass filter ([WebKit 179411](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=179411); [WebKit 192932](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=192932)). Two consequences follow. Absolute dB is untrustworthy, so ratios, statistics and classifier tags are the usable features. And every echo time must be measured from the direct-path peak inside the same recording, never from the `play()` call.

## Audio geolocation is continental; anchored sources are local

The literature splits by scale. Mains-hum ENF identifies the grid at about **92% accuracy across 9 grids** ([arXiv 2502.10011](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.10011v1)). Continental Europe is a single synchronous grid, though, and intra-grid localisation needs about 20 dB ENF SNR where phone audio gives 9–15 dB ([arXiv 2105.00668](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.00668)). ENF therefore has zero spatial value in Vincennes and at most serves as a timestamp or replay check. Learned audio geolocation (AG-CLIP, GeoCLAP, PSM) is continental. Off-the-shelf GeoCLAP scored a **6,856 km median error**, close to random ([arXiv 2505.18726](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18726v2)). Telling apart which of six European cities a recording came from manages only **50–56%** ([arXiv 1905.00979](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00979)). Fingerprinting does reach room level, with SurroundSense at **87% across 51 stores** using several sensors ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2009/09/090924093345.htm)) and Batphone at **69% across 33 rooms** ([Tarzia et al. 2011](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~pdinda/Papers/mobisys11.pdf)). Batphone fell to **40%** in an independent retest and "fails in the presence of chatters" ([arXiv 1809.00531](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.00531)). Every fingerprinting system also needs a prior survey of the area, which is impossible in contested ground.

The usable signal is therefore what `backend/soundscape.py` already exploits: rare sound sources tied to fixed places on the map. In Vincennes, generic traffic is everywhere and nearly uninformative. Surface rail, trams, ringing belfries, fountains and park interiors are spatially sparse, and that sparseness is what makes them informative. Strategic noise maps exist at **5 m resolution** from Bruitparif ([methodology note](https://www.bruitparif.fr/pages/En-tete/800%20Le%20bruit%20en%20%C3%8Ele-de-France/300%20carto-air-bruit-en-idf/700%20Principaux%20r%C3%A9sultats%20et%20statistiques/Note%20m%C3%A9thodologique%20et%20de%20r%C3%A9sultats%20cartographie%20air-bruit.pdf)), but the national open shapefiles **exclude the Métropole du Grand Paris** ([data.gouv.fr](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/cartes-de-bruit-strategiques-des-reseaux-routiers-et-ferroviaires-non-concedes-directive-europeenne-2002-49-ce)). A Vincennes Lden raster is therefore not one download away. It would also add little. CNOSSOS map error is about **0–4 dB(A)** ([PMC12899886](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12899886/)), phones read about **4 dB off** a reference meter ([Sensors 2016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5087480/)), and only 3–4 of 10 tested iOS apps stayed within ±2 dB ([Kardous & Shaw 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25236152/)). Absolute-level matching should stay off.

## Passive outdoor ranging is factor-of-two at best, but it is free

Physics limits single-mic outdoor distance estimation. Steady traffic is a line source losing **3 dB per doubling of distance (4.5 dB over soft ground)**, and a single vehicle is a point source losing 6 dB ([FHWA](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/environMent/noise/regulations_and_guidance/polguide/polguide02.cfm); [FHWA barriers](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/Environment/noise/noise_barriers/design_construction/design/design03.cfm)). A 3 dB phone error therefore equals a factor of two in road distance. Level alone is only good for "next to a main road (<50 m)" versus "deep in a park or courtyard (>200 m)". Better features don't depend on calibration. One is the spread between loud and quiet moments (L10 − L90): small next to steady traffic, large where discrete pass-bys stand out over a quiet background. Another is the band ratio between birdsong and traffic frequencies. Air absorbs about **0.56 dB/100 m at 1 kHz versus 9–10 dB/100 m at 4 kHz** ([CCRMA](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp/Air_Absorption.html)), so the high/low traffic band ratio falls with distance, though calibrating it is research work. Single-mic pass-by analysis reliably estimates vehicle speed (about **7.9 km/h mean error**, [arXiv 2204.04013](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.04013)) but no published system reports closest-approach distance. A heuristic of d ≈ v·(−3 dB width)/2, assuming a 30–50 km/h city speed, gives roughly ±50% and only works for isolated vehicles.

Per-class detection ranges set the radius of each audibility term. Bells reach 110–125 dB at the tower ([Svantek](https://svantek.com/services/church-bells-noise/)), yet measured effective coverage in a Catalan valley was only **200–350 m** ([Studia Universitatis Hereditati 2024](https://www.hippocampus.si/ISSN/2350-5443/2-2024/SUH_2350-5443.12(2)65-84.pdf)). A logistic whose 50% point sits at 400–600 m in town, with a soft "not heard" penalty, is a defensible placeholder. A train behaves as a finite line source, with train SEL predictable within about ±6 dB(A) from speed ([IoA](https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/publications/RA%20HOOD%20PR%20WILLIAMS%20KM%20COLLINS%20RJ%20GREER%20THE%20CALCULATION%20OF%20TRAIN%20NOISE.pdf)). In Vincennes, though, RER A and metro 1 run largely underground, so the OSM `tunnel=yes` flag matters more than any propagation model. Birds are weak in late November, and BirdNET's recall is only **0.24–0.52** ([global assessment](https://hal.inrae.fr/hal-04633976v1/document)). That is why "no birds heard" must never count strongly against a park. Aircraft over east Paris fly at **1,500–4,500 m** ([Assemblée nationale](https://questions.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/13/questions/QANR5L13QE23660.pdf)), constant across the demo box, so they carry no position information. Gunfire crack-to-thump timing ranges the shooter, not the listener ([arXiv 1905.13313](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.13313)), which belongs on the casualty card as a safety note and not in the localiser. Learned single-channel distance models reach about **0.5 m error indoors** ([Neri et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.17514)), but they depend on reverberation, which outdoor scenes lack.

## Phone sonar sees walls within six metres and is audible far beyond

Active sensing is the strongest cue audio has for the immediate surroundings, and it has a hard range limit. BatMapper measured **1–2 cm error at the 80th percentile for walls 0.5–3.5 m away** and stated that "objects more than 6 meters away create very weak echoes, which can be ignored" ([BatMapper](https://zhoubinwy.github.io/pdf/batmapper.pdf)). Its room-geometry results (<0.3 m error at the 80th percentile) needed two mics, the IMU and arm sweeps, and failed in irregular rooms or rooms larger than 8×8 m. With one co-located speaker and mic, "distance measures have reasonable accuracy, while direction measurement is often subject to large measurement error" ([arXiv 1612.05793](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.05793)). Speaker ringing lasts about **13.8 ms**, and echoes arrive about **40 dB below** the direct path ([arXiv 1809.00531](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.00531)). The first 0.3–2.4 m of range is therefore blind unless a free-air reference IR is subtracted. No peer-reviewed phone-only measurement of ranging to outdoor facades 10–50 m away turned up in the research. Spherical spreading alone costs an 18 m round trip about 25 dB against a 1 m wall, which buries such echoes in street noise.

The current 800 Hz–12 kHz sweep is a sound choice for physics. Its bandwidth gives about 1.5 cm theoretical range resolution, and it stays in the band phone speakers actually reproduce. Near-ultrasound would be quieter, but the team's ISO 9613-1 extrapolation puts absorption around **0.5 dB/m at 20 kHz**, so it only works at room scale. Phones also roll off sharply above about 20 kHz ([arXiv 1809.00531](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.00531)). From one ping, then, the system can expect three things: an enclosed-versus-open label, a nearest-surface distance when that surface is under about 6 m, and a reflection-richness feature. It cannot expect room shape or "which facade".

Detectability is the real cost. Phone speakers produce about **78 dB at 1 m** ([PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/Loud-louder-LOUDEST-Here-are-the-phones-with-the-loudest-speakers-so-far-in-2015_id74023)). By the team's spreading-plus-absorption calculation, a 2 kHz component stays above ambient for about **150–370 m on a quiet night** and 30–90 m with light background noise. A 19 kHz chirp is inaudible to most adults beyond about a metre, since only **52% of under-30s hear 20 kHz** and fewer than 20% of over-31s hear 18–20 kHz ([PMC9450601](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9450601/)). A microphone could still pick it up at 70–90 m. Battlefield acoustic sensing is real and fielded: the Twister drone's sensor detects artillery at 5 km ([heise](https://heise.de/-10279458)), and NATO documents acoustic sensor networks in Ukraine ([NATO JALLC 2026](https://nllp.jallc.nato.int/iks/sharing%20public/20260109_(nu)_%20uas_acoustic_sensor_systems_for_artillery_and_drone_threat_detection.pdf)). A coherent, repeated, known-form sweep is exactly what a matched filter finds. The ping should be off by default, sent once, short (a few ms, not 0.5 s) and quiet, with a silent mode. The TTS read-back over the loudspeaker is a bigger signature than the chirp, so an earpiece is the first OPSEC fix.

## The twin predicts the scene; audio only has to pick a category

The 3D model should come from the digital twin, with audio choosing among predicted scenes. Street acoustics follow geometry in a regular way. Across 144 simulated real streets, **RT30 sat mostly between 1 and 3 s** and rose with facade height, sidewalk width and enclosure. Attenuation was linear in log-distance with R² ≥ 0.977, and the ODEON simulation matched measurements to **within 15%** ([Wu, Kang & Jin 2017](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10054793/1/Hupeng%20WU%20-%20street-parameter-paper-whiterose2.pdf)). T30 and EDT increase with H/W ([Lee & Kang 2015](https://dx.doi.org/10.3813/AAA.918806)), and courtyards can exceed 4 s ([Yang, Kang & Kim 2017](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10054786/1/Manuscript_Apartment%20RT.pdf)). Ray tracing 25,600 cells is out of scope for a day. Marching 8–16 rays per cell over the 5 m IGN raster to get facade distances, mean height, H/W and openness is a numpy job of minutes, though. That captures the variables the regressions say matter, and it produces both the "real candidate scenes" on the pipeline page and a predicted setting class and expected first-echo delay (2d/343 s) per cell. Matching then happens on 3–4 categories through a confusion matrix, which is honest given ±0.2 s T60 error and ±15% simulation error.

Fusion must be robust, because naive products of overconfident terms are the main way grid localisers fail. Neural classifiers are systematically overconfident, and temperature scaling is the standard post-hoc fix ([Guo et al. 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html)). Raising a likelihood to a power ζ ≤ 1 gives robustness to a misspecified model ([Miller & Dunson 2019](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1506.06101)). The practical form is L = ε + (1−ε)·p^τ. Suggested starting values: τ ≈ 0.5 for sound events, 0.7 for the setting class, 1 for spoken bearings and sun, and ε ≈ 0.1, so one wrong YAMNet tag can cut a cell at most tenfold. Repeated windows of the same event should collapse into one observation, and YAMNet and AST scores for one event should be combined by max, never multiplied. Batphone's per-band 5th-percentile spectrum is a clean way to separate the stationary background (road hum versus quiet park) from transients worth tagging ([Tarzia et al. 2011](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~pdinda/Papers/mobisys11.pdf)).

Minimising questions is mostly a design problem, and the theory backs the current selector. For noisy twenty-questions with entropy loss, greedy one-step expected-entropy reduction is **Bayes-optimal over the whole sequence** ([Jedynak, Frazier & Sznitman 2012](https://www.cis.jhu.edu/~bruno/JedynakPaper-02-2011.pdf)). `backend/questions.py` should therefore extend its action set rather than change its rule. A yes/no answer carries at most 1 bit. Shrinking a 300 m radius to 60 m is an area ratio of 25, about 4.6 bits, so it needs at least five perfect binary answers. A clock-bearing answer has 12 outcomes, up to about 3.6 bits. Weighting EIG by attention cost makes passive listening and an automatic setting estimate nearly free and makes spoken questions expensive. That lets the system stay silent while the buddy works and re-fuse every 60 s. Silence also carries information: with 3-minute headways, hearing no train for five minutes counts against cells next to a surface line. The best lever of all is the first utterance. Doctrine already defines a voice resection, which takes azimuths to 2–3 known points, a polar plot from one known point plus a distance, and terrain association ([TC 3-25.26](https://armyrotc.mst.edu/media/academic/armyrotc/documents/manualsadpadrpfmetc/TC%203-25.26.pdf)). A trained template ("three landmarks with clock directions, sun, one distance, the feature I'm on, what I hear") front-loads most of the information. Its output maps directly onto MEDEVAC Line 1, which asks for "as accurate a grid as possible" ([UN 9-line](https://indico.un.org/event/1003005/attachments/2107/6975/13%209line.pdf)).

## Conclusion

The research inverts the intuitive framing. Audio is not a locator. It is a filter whose job is to make the first spoken resection unambiguous, and its value comes almost entirely from sound sources that are rare on the map and from geometry the twin already knows. That makes the IGN height raster, not the microphone, the source of the "3D model of the surroundings". The microphone only has to say "canyon, not courtyard, wall at 4 m, train within 400 m". The approach also appears to be new: no published system uses a noise or acoustic map as a likelihood for GPS-free outdoor positioning ([NoiseSPY](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220134386_NoiseSPY_A_Real-Time_Mobile_Phone_Platform_for_Urban_Noise_Monitoring_and_Mapping) and its successors run the inverse problem). That is a genuine originality claim for the pitch, but there is no published accuracy to borrow, so any number Pothenot quotes has to come from its own walk around the Château.

Two items are open risks rather than known quantities. The detection range of fielded military acoustic sensors against a short phone chirp has no open-source answer, which argues for a silent-by-default posture. And no blind reverberation model has been validated on close-talk phone speech or on rural and battlefield scenes. Until it is, the setting classifier should carry a low τ and the chirp should be optional evidence, never the deciding term.
